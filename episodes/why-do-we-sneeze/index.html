Why Do We Sneeze?

Ages 5–10 | A sneeze is a burst of air that helps your body clear things that bother the inside of your nose.

Find out how a tiny tickle sends a message to your brain and gets your breathing muscles moving. Find out what happens when some people step into bright light.

Try this at home: The next time you hear a sneeze, talk about which came first, the breath in or the puff out.

Website, all episodes and transcripts: https://dadhowdoesitwork.com/

Quick answer: Why Do We Sneeze?

A sneeze is your body's super-fast puff of air for clearing out a speck of dust or anything else that tickles the inside of your nose. Tiny nerves in your nose send a message to your brain, which makes you take a big breath, closes your throat for a split second while your chest and belly muscles squeeze, and then blasts the air out through your nose and mouth. It is a reflex, so it happens without you deciding, and some people even sneeze when they step into bright light.

Topic: Your Body · Published 19 November 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. How is a sneeze like a quick puff of air on a scrap of paper sitting on a desk? Why wouldn't a gentle, ordinary breath do the same job?
  2. When a tiny speck gets past the sticky lining inside your nose, what message do the nerve endings send, and what does the brain tell your body to do first?
  3. Some people sneeze when they walk from a dark place into bright daylight. What two nerves sit close together, and what does the episode say the bright light accidentally does?

Host 2: How does light cause a sneeze though? That seems so backwards.
