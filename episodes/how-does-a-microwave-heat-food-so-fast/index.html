How Does a Microwave Heat Food So Fast?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

You put cold pizza into the microwave. You press a button. Thirty seconds later it’s hot.

But the plate is sometimes still cool. And the middle of the food can still be cold.

So what is the microwave actually doing in there?

No fire. No heater. Just that humming sound and the spinning plate.

Something invisible is happening inside the food…and once you see it, the microwave suddenly makes a lot more sense.

Quick answer: How Does a Microwave Heat Food So Fast?

A microwave oven sends invisible waves into the food that make the tiny water molecules in the outer layer of the food wiggle back and forth extremely fast. All that jiggling makes heat, which then spreads inward, so the food warms up quickly.

Topic: Home & Everyday Stuff · Published 6 March 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. What tiny things inside your food start to "dance" when the microwave is on?
  2. Why does the plate often stay cool while the pizza on it gets hot?
  3. Why does the glass plate inside the microwave spin around?
