Why Do Bites and Cuts Feel Worse in the Shower?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

Ages 5–10 | Why do bites and cuts feel worse in the shower? You step into the shower with a mosquito bite or a tiny cut.

It was barely bothering you before.

Then the water touches it, and suddenly it itches or stings.

But why would clean water make it feel worse?

Is the water doing something to your skin?

Or was the bite already sending a signal?

And why does scratching feel good, even when it makes the itching come back?

Liked this one? Please rate the show on Apple Podcasts or Spotify. It helps other curious families find us.

Quick answer: Why Do Bites and Cuts Feel Worse in the Shower?

Water softens the skin and spreads over the small wound, and warm water sends more blood to the area. That wakes up the nerves around the bite or cut, so they send stronger itch or sting signals, even though the water itself is harmless.

Topic: Your Body · Published 19 September 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. Does a mosquito inject "itch juice"? What does your own body do instead?
  2. How is a bite in the shower like a smoke detector with the dial turned all the way up?
  3. Why does soap sting a scrape? Does the sting mean it's killing germs?

Host 2: Well, it comes back to that alarm network we talked about. See, healthy skin is like a thick protective blanket covering your nerve endings. When you scrape your knee, you literally physically scrape away that protective blanket.

Host 2: Yeah, exactly. And that leaves your bare nerve endings completely exposed to the open air.

Host 1: Oh wow. So when the soap and water touch the scrape, there's literally no shield left. The bare sensors just panic.

Host 2: Exactly.
