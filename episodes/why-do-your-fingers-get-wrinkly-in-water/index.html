Why Do Your Fingers Get Wrinkly in Water?

You’re sitting in the bath, and suddenly your fingers look completely different. Why do they get wrinkly while the rest of your skin mostly doesn’t? You might think your fingers are just soaking up water like little sponges. But then why are your fingertips and toes the ones that wrinkle the most? And why are your nerves involved in something as simple as bath wrinkles? It turns out your body is actually helping create those wrinkles. And there may be a surprisingly useful reason they appear when things get wet.

Quick answer: Why Do Your Fingers Get Wrinkly in Water?

Wrinkly fingers aren't just soggy skin. After a few minutes in water, your nerves tell the blood vessels in your fingertips to shrink, which pulls the skin into wrinkles. Scientists think the wrinkles may help us grip wet things, a bit like tyre treads.

Topic: Your Body · Published 30 August 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. Is wrinkly skin just soaked-up water like a sponge? What clue in the episode shows it isn't?
  2. What do the tiny blood vessels under your skin do to make the wrinkles?
  3. How are wrinkly fingers like the bottom of your sneakers?
