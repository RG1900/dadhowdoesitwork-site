Why Do Companies Need More Money to Grow?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

Ages 5–10 | Why do companies need more money to grow? You build a lemonade stand, and people love it.

So why would you need someone else's money? Shouldn't a good business pay for itself?

Then you want to open another stand, buy better tools, and tell more people about it.

Suddenly, the money you made isn't enough.

That's when startup funding starts to make sense.

But it isn't free money: someone gives you money now because they believe your business could grow much bigger later.

Liked this one? Please rate the show on Apple Podcasts or Spotify. It helps other curious families find us.

Quick answer: Why Do Companies Need More Money to Grow?

To grow, a company needs money before it earns it back: for new workers, machines, shops or factories. It can borrow money, or sell small pieces of itself, called shares, to investors who hope the company will grow.

Topic: Money & Business · Published 14 May 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. Why might a company that is selling lots still ask investors for money?
  2. In the pizza-dough example, what does the uncle get in return for his $200?
  3. If the lemonade stand fails, does the uncle get his money back? Why not?
