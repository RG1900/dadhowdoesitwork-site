How Do Fish Even Breathe Underwater?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

You’re watching a fish and suddenly it hits you…

it never comes up for air.

Its mouth keeps opening and closing like it’s chewing water.

But water isn’t air… so what is it actually doing?

Then you notice those little flaps on the sides moving too.

Something is flowing through its body all the time.

And somehow… it’s getting oxygen without ever leaving the water.

Quick answer: How Do Fish Even Breathe Underwater?

Fish breathe through gills, thin feathery parts on the sides of their heads. Water flows in through the mouth and out over the gills, which take the oxygen dissolved in the water and pass it into the fish's blood.
