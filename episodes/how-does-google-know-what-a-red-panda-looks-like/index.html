How Does Google Know What a Red Panda Looks Like?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

You search for “red panda,” and the right photos appear almost instantly.

But most of those pictures do not have the words “red panda” written on them.

So how does Google know what is actually inside each photo?

Does it see the animal, or is it using clues hidden around the image?

And how can it tell the difference between a bear, a costume, and a cartoon character?

It all begins with tiny colored squares and a huge picture-sorting system.

Quick answer: How Does Google Know What a Red Panda Looks Like?

Google's computers learned from millions of pictures that people had already labelled, like 'red panda'. By comparing patterns of shapes and colours, the computer learned what red pandas usually look like, so it can recognise one in a new photo.

Topic: Machines & Tech · Published 31 July 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. If you zoom way into a photo, what does the computer "see" instead of a red panda?
  2. How do labelled pictures, like sticky notes, help a computer learn what a red panda looks like?
  3. Why might a computer mistake a kid in a bear costume for a real bear?
