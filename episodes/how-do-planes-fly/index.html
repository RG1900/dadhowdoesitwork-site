How Do Planes Fly?

You look up and see a huge airplane crossing the sky.

It’s made of metal, seats, people, and bags, so it should be too heavy to fly.

So why doesn’t it just fall straight down?

When it moves fast, the air starts rushing around the wings.

The wings push the air down, and the air pushes back up.

It’s the same feeling as when you stick your hand out the car window and it lifts.

So maybe the sky isn’t empty after all.

Maybe the airplane is riding on the air itself.

Quick answer: How Do Planes Fly?

A plane's wings are shaped and tilted so that, as the plane moves fast, they push air downward. The air pushes the wing back up, and that push is called lift. The engines don't lift the plane; their job is to push it forward fast enough for the wings to work.

Topic: Machines & Tech · Published 14 February 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. What happens to your hand when you tilt it up out of a car window? How is a plane's wing like your hand?
  2. The engines don't lift the plane up. What is their job?
  3. The wing pushes air down. What does the air do back to the wing?

Host 1: It's a common idea. But they're not. They point backward. Their only job is to push the plane forward really, really fast.

Host 2: They're just there to make it go.

Host 1: They provide the speed. Without that speed, the wings can't do their job. No speed means no lift. It's that simple.

Host 2: So if the wing pushes the air down...

Host 2: And you have lift from the wings, which is trying to push the plane up toward the sky.

Host 1: Right. And flying is just managing that battle. If lift is stronger than gravity, the plane climbs.

Host 2: If they're equal?

Host 1: You stay level, just cruising along.

Host 2: And if gravity is stronger than lift, you start to sink.

Host 1: You descend. It's all a balancing act.

Host 2: I want to go back to the engines for a second, because this is a really important point to get right. So you're saying if you took the wings off a plane but kept those super powerful engines, it wouldn't fly.

Host 1: Not even an inch off the ground. It would just be an incredibly fast, very dangerous car.

Host 2: Wow. So it's a team effort. The engines make the run.

Host 1: And the wings make the jump. You absolutely need both.

Host 1: Well, in a way you can. Wings aren't totally solid. They have small moving parts on the back edges.

Host 2: Like flaps?

Host 1: Exactly. Flaps, ailerons, elevators. By moving these little parts up or down, the pilot changes the shape of the wing just a little bit.

Host 2: And changing the shape changes how the air pushes on it.

Host 1: Precisely. It's like you're steering the plane by shaping the air around it. You make the air push you left or right or up or down.

Host 2: That's amazing. Let's connect all this to something we've all felt: takeoff.

Host 1: Okay. First, you hear the engines get really loud, right? A big roar.

Host 2: And you feel it. The rumbling stops.

Host 2: So to wrap it all up then, the simple formula is...
