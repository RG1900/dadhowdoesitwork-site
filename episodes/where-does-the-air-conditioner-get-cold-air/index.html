Where Does the Air Conditioner Get Cold Air?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

You press the button, and cold air starts blowing out.

But where did that cold air come from?

Was it hiding inside the air conditioner?

And if the room air was hot a second ago, how can it come back cold?

The strange part is that the air conditioner might not be bringing new air at all.

It may be taking the same room air, removing heat from it, and sending it back colder.

So the real mystery is not where the cold air was.

It is where the heat went.

Quick answer: Where Does the Air Conditioner Get Cold Air?

An air conditioner doesn't make cold; it moves heat. A special liquid inside picks up heat from the room's air and carries it outside, where the outdoor unit releases it, so the air blown back into the room is cooler.

Topic: Home & Everyday Stuff · Published 4 June 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. Does the air conditioner make cold air, or does it take something away from the air? What does it take?
  2. How is a cold metal spoon from the freezer like the cold coil inside the air conditioner?
  3. Why would a room get warmer, not colder, if you put both air conditioner boxes inside the same room?

Host 1: Exactly. It feels totally supernatural. I honestly used to wonder if there was just, like, a secret box of winter hiding inside the wall. Like, where in the world is it finding all that freezing wind?

Host 1: Yeah, exactly, like a mini freezer.

Host 2: But if you actually look at that white box on the wall, I mean, it is tiny. It is way too small to hold enough cold air to cool down an entire room all day.

Host 1: That is a really good point. It does not actually have a secret freezer tucked inside.

Host 2: No, definitely not.

Host 1: So what is it actually doing then? Because I always just pictured it blowing cold air at me.

Host 2: Well, the first thing to know is that it is not just blowing air out. Inside that box, there is a spinning fan and its main job is to actually pull air in.

Host 1: Wait, really? It is pulling air in?

Host 2: Yeah, it aggressively pulls the warm stuffy air from your room right up into the machine.

Host 1: Hold on, let me make sure I am getting this. Are you saying that the amazing freezing cold air hitting my face is literally the exact same hot air that was just floating around in my room?

Host 2: That is exactly what is happening. The machine just breathes in your hot room air, does something to it, and breathes it back out.

Host 1: Oh, wow. That completely flips it around for me. But I mean, how does the hot air suddenly become cold inside that little plastic box?

Host 2: So this is where we have to change how we think about it. The air conditioner is not a cold-making machine. It is actually a heat-moving delivery system.

Host 1: A heat-moving delivery system. Yeah. Like a delivery person.

Host 2: Exactly like that.

Host 1: But wait, usually a delivery person brings something to you, like a pizza. So instead of bringing a pizza to my house, they are, what, taking the heat out of my room?

Host 2: You nailed it. It is not bringing cold in, it is taking heat away. Because, scientifically speaking, cold is really just the feeling of having less heat.

Host 1: And it's such a wild way to think about it. It is a heat-removing machine.

Host 2: Right. So when the warm room air gets pulled in, it passes over the very cold metal part inside called a coil.

Host 1: Okay, a [unclear] metal coil. And that coil steals the heat.

Host 2: Yes. The heat naturally jumps from the warm air into the cold metal.

Host 1: Oh, I know what this is like. Yeah. It is just like grabbing a super cold metal spoon right out of the freezer.

Host 2: Oh, that is a great example.

Host 1: Yeah, like when you touch the spoon, your finger starts to ache and feel freezing. But the spoon is not pushing cold into you. It is literally stealing the warmth right out of your skin.

Host 2: Exactly. Inside the AC, that cold metal coil acts just like the spoon. It steals the heat right out of the air. And with less heat, the air feels colder when the fan blows it back out into the room.

Host 1: That makes so much sense. But wait, if the heat is being stolen from the air, where does it go? I mean, it cannot just vanish.

Host 2: No, it definitely cannot. And this is where our delivery person comes back in. Inside the machine, there is this special stuff called refrigerant.

Host 1: Refrigerant, okay, I have heard of that.

Host 2: Yeah, it is basically the special delivery person inside the pipes. It flows through these closed loops, kind of switching between being a liquid and a gas, and it picks up all that stolen heat from the metal coil.

Host 1: Got it. So the refrigerant grabs the heat. Then what does it do with it?

Host 2: Then it carries that heat completely out of your room. It travels through the pipes all the way to that big loud box that sits outside your house or apartment window.

Host 1: Is that why standing near the outside AC unit always feels so horribly hot?

Host 2: Yes, exactly.

Host 1: Because it is literally blowing warm air on you.

Host 2: You got it. There's a big fan in that outside box and its whole job is to blow outdoor air over the pipes so it can throw your room's heat away into the outside world.

Host 1: That is amazing. Okay, I have a fun thought experiment for you then.

Host 2: Oh, I love these. Lay it on me.

Host 1: What if we took the indoor white box and that big noisy outdoor box and we put them both inside the exact same closed bedroom. Would the room get super freezing?

Host 2: You know, it seems like it should, right? But no, the room would actually get warmer.

Host 1: Wait, warmer? How does that work?

Host 2: Well, think about it. The machine would just be picking up heat from one corner of the room and immediately dumping it back out into another corner.

Host 1: Oh, right. Because the outdoor box is in the room now, the heat never actually leaves.

Host 2: Exactly. Plus, running the machine itself makes extra heat. The motors get warm, so you are just shuffling heat around and adding motor heat on top of it.

Host 1: That perfectly proves why you absolutely have to carry the heat completely outside.

Host 2: Spot on. It has to go outside.

Host 1: Okay, one last question. Since the AC is just recycling that exact same room air over and over, does it ever bring in fresh air from outside?

Host 2: That is a really common misunderstanding, actually. Most home air conditioners do not get any fresh air at all.

Host 1: Wow, really? So cold air does not actually mean fresh air?

Host 2: No, it does not. If you are sitting in a closed room with your friends, you know, playing games, the room might be nice and cold, but it is just the same recycled air.

Host 1: Which means it can still get super stuffy.

Host 2: Exactly. So sometimes you still need to just crack a window to get actual fresh oxygen in there.

Host 1: That is so good to know. Well, to wrap this up, the air conditioner does not have a secret freezer hiding inside. Definitely not. It just takes your warm room air, uses a delivery system to carry the heat outside and gives you back that exact same air minus the heat.

Host 2: Yep, that is the whole process. It is a bit less like magic, but honestly it is even cooler when you know how it actually works.

Host 1: I totally agree.
