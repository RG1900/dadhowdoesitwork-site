Why Is the Sky Blue?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Watch on YouTube (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

You look up and the whole sky is blue.

But the Sun doesn’t look blue at all. It looks white or yellow.

So where is that blue coming from?

Sunlight travels through the air, and suddenly the light starts bouncing around.

Some colors spread everywhere. Others keep going straight.

It’s like tiny air molecules turning blue light into ping-pong balls and sending them in every direction.

That scattered blue glow is what reaches your eyes.

And without air, the sky wouldn’t be blue at all.

Topic: Space & Sky · Published 14 February 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. What two "ingredients" does the sky need to look blue?
  2. In the episode, red light is like a bowling ball and blue light is like a ping pong ball. Which one bounces all over the sky, and why does that make the sky blue?
  3. If you stood on the Moon in the daytime, what colour would the sky be? Why?
