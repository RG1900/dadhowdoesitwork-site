How Does the Remote Talk to the TV?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

A TV remote feels obvious until you ask the basic question: how does the TV know what you pressed from across the room?

A remote turns each button press into a coded signal. In many standard remotes, that signal is infrared light, which your eyes cannot see but the TV’s sensor can detect.

Each button sends a different pattern. The TV reads that pattern, matches it to a command, and changes the volume, channel, or power state.

After this episode, you’ll understand what is actually happening between the remote and the TV, and why pointing, blocked sensors, and weak batteries matter.

Quick answer: How Does the Remote Talk to the TV?

A TV remote sends flashes of invisible infrared light from the little bulb at its front. Each button sends a different pattern of flashes, like a secret code, and a sensor on the TV reads the code and does what you asked.

Topic: Machines & Tech · Published 12 June 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. The remote's little bulb flashes a light we can't see. What is that light called?
  2. What happens under a button when you press it? How is it like a drawbridge?
  3. Why doesn't the remote work when it's buried under a thick blanket?
