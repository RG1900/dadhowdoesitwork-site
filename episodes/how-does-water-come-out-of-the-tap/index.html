How Does Water Come Out of the Tap?

Listen now
Spotify (opens in a new tab)Apple Podcasts (opens in a new tab)Other apps (opens in a new tab)

You turn the tap, and water just appears. It feels like it was waiting behind the wall. But where was it one second before? And why does the toilet suddenly refill after you flush? The water isn’t being made inside your house. It is moving through hidden pipes, gates, pressure, and gravity. And the more you follow it, the less it feels like magic.

Quick answer: How Does Water Come Out of the Tap?

Water travels from reservoirs and treatment plants through big underground pipes. It is pushed along under pressure, often from water towers or pumps, so when you open the tap, the pressure pushes water out.

Topic: Home & Everyday Stuff · Published 18 June 2026

Talk about it after listening

  1. If the pipes are always full of water, why doesn't it pour out until you turn the tap?
  2. What pushes the water out of the tap? (Hint: think about a water tower.)
  3. Where does the water go after it goes down the drain, and why doesn't it mix with the clean water?

Host 1: Okay, which brings up a pretty logical question. If those pipes behind the wall are constantly full of water, why doesn't it just pour out onto the floor all the time?

Host 1: So that constant push is called pressure. Going back to your hallway analogy, the crowd of people isn't just standing there casually, right?

Host 2: No, they are actively pushing forward against the door.

Host 1: So the absolute second you open it, the people right at the front exit instantly because they were already there being pushed from behind.

Host 2: Exactly. You need both. You need an open path and pressure to make it flow.

Host 1: Oh, so it's just gravity doing the work. And after it flushes, you always hear that, uh, that long shh sound for about a minute.

Host 2: Yeah, that sound is just another valve opening up. It's letting fresh pressurized water from the wall refill the empty tank.

Host 1: But the tank has a really clever way of knowing when to stop filling, right? So it doesn't just overflow everywhere.

Host 2: It does. Inside there's a lightweight plastic piece that literally floats on top of the water. As the fresh water fills up the tank, the float rises higher and higher.

Host 1: Like a little boat.

Host 2: Just like a boat. And when the water reaches the very top, that floating piece physically pushes the valve shut.

Host 1: Okay, so now we have all this clean water coming in.

Host 2: It would be a huge mess. No, your house actually has two completely separate water road systems.

Host 2: Yeah, one system strictly brings clean water in and a totally different system takes used water out. They never cross paths.

Host 1: Got it. So the clean water is pushed in by pressure, but the dirty water leaves through the drains. You know those dark little holes in the bottom of your sink, your bathtub, or the toilet?

Host 2: You got it. Gravity just pulls the dirty water down and away from the house. It gently falls down into bigger underground pipes called sewers.
